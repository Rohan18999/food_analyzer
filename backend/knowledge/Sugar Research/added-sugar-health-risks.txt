Research consistently links high added sugar consumption to adverse health outcomes including obesity, type 2 diabetes, dental cavities, and metabolic syndrome.

Key findings from nutritional research:
- Added sugars provide empty calories without essential nutrients
- Liquid sugars (sodas, juices) are particularly associated with weight gain
- The American Heart Association recommends no more than 25g added sugar per day for women and 36g for men
- Products with more than 10g added sugars per serving should be consumed sparingly

Reading labels for "added sugars" (separate from total sugars) helps identify products with significant sugar content. Natural sugars in whole fruits are metabolized differently than added sugars in processed foods.
